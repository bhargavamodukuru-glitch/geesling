**Google Places: what was researched and decided (2026-10-20)** — so nobody repeats it.
  - Pricing (per 1,000 calls; first 1,000/month free for the Enterprise tiers): Nearby/Text Search Pro $32 (5,000 free), Enterprise $35 (adds rating and review count), Enterprise + Atmosphere $40 (adds reviews and `editorialSummary`). Nearby Search returns at most 20 places, no paging. Our guide's Text Search calls already sit at the $40 tier because the field mask includes `editorialSummary`. Sources: developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing and .../places/web-service/data-fields.
  - A map-wide places layer would cost about $0.04 per map load (~$80/month for 20 testers at 5 loads a day). Dropped by the user.
  - Google has no opening date for recently opened places (`openingDate` is only set for FUTURE_OPENING), so a "NEW" label would be a heuristic (few reviews, all recent). Dropped.
  - **Unresolved compliance risks in what is live** (found while researching; not fixed): (1) iOS uses Apple Maps, and Google's terms bar showing Places content on a non-Google map, so walk pins with Google names and ratings on iPhone break that rule (fix: use Google Maps on iOS; `app.config.ts` already supports an iOS key); (2) we store Google content. **2a fixed (2026-10-20):** the unused `places` table (369 rows of names, addresses, ratings, hours and summaries, written by the guide, publicly readable through the API) is no longer written (`cachePlaces` removed from the guide) and its rows were deleted by migration `20261020120000_stop_places_cache.sql`; the table stays, empty. **2b still open:** every saved or shared walk keeps a snapshot of each stop's Google fields in `walks.stops`, and Google's policy says not to store Places content beyond place IDs (I could not read the exact exceptions). Fixing it means storing place IDs plus our own text and fetching names and ratings when a walk is opened, which costs Google calls per stop per view; price that before deciding. Note: `wip/hidden-eggs-gta` (Hidden Eggs) relied on that places cache, so it needs a different design if revived; and its migrations (`20261020000000`, `...0001`) sort before `20261020120000`, so applying them later needs `supabase db push --include-all`; (3) Places data shown without a Google map (walk cards, detail sheet) needs the Google logo, not just text. See developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/policies.

**Long outings (2026-10-20, deployed).** A user prompt ("in the city for about 12 hours today… hotpot for dinner") got 2 stops. Causes: the chat chip was on "Any time", so no time budget was sent and the guide used its 6-hour default, and it was 9:04 pm. Now `fill.ts` reads durations written in the message (`statedDuration`, used only when the chip is "Any time"), and caps the window at 2 am Toronto (`minutesLeftTonight`), telling the guide to say the rest would be tomorrow. Outings of 6+ hours get 11 turns and medium effort. Retest: evening request = 3 stops (hotpot, café, park) with an honest note about the cutoff; same prompt from 10 am = only 4 stops ending ~3:45 pm, with the hotpot at 2:15 pm instead of dinner, and the reply took 70 s. **Structural limit:** `buildStops` lays place stops back to back from the start time (only events wait for their start), so a meal can't be placed at dinner time unless earlier stops fill the day exactly. **Fixed the same day (guide and web deployed; walk card checked live on /walk/5imxcwndcf):** present_walk stops take an optional `arrive_after` (Toronto HH:MM), and `buildStops` waits until then (`daytime.ts`, max 6 h wait). Hops into a new part of the day (or a timed meal) may take 40 min instead of 20. The fill check counts stays and travel only, not waiting. If the guide runs out of turns, the walk it last built and we sent back is presented instead of "Tell me a bit more" (trimmed before the first long hop, with a plain intro). **Parts of the day and typed budgets (2026-10-21, deployed).** The user's definitions: night = 5 pm onward ("date night", "tonight", "friday night"), evening = 4 pm onward, afternoon = 12–3:59 pm, morning = 8 am–noon (`dayPart` in `when.ts`). **Budget ranges (2026-10-21, deployed).** Budgets are a range for everyone together (`Budget {min, limit, people}` in `budget.ts`): "under $50" = $0–$50; a typed range ("$30-$60", "$30 to $60", "between $30 and $60") uses its min and max; "each"/"pp" multiplies both by the people. The final walk must not be able to exceed the max (places at the top of Google's range) and, when there's a minimum, must not come in under it (places at the bottom of their range); the guide is told the range and sent back with the reason if it lands outside. The request body also accepts `budgetMin` (the planner sends none, so $0). Local test: "date night, under $50" gave $20–$40 for two over three stops; "date night, $30-$60, leslieville" gave Elviras at 5 pm for $40–$60. Afternoon now ends at 4 pm everywhere (walk-card headers, server hop rules, the guide's long-outing rule), matching the user's definitions.

**Routes and runners (2026-10-21, deployed).** Everything people see says "route" instead of "walk" (buttons, loader, shared page, share text, the goose's instructions); "Walk" stays only where it means walking between stops. Internal names (`Walk` type, `walks` table, `present_walk` tool, `/walk/<id>` URLs) are unchanged so existing share links work. Runners: `run.ts` reads a distance ("15k", "10 km", "10 mile", "half marathon", "marathon"; 1–50 km) when there's a running word, a pace ("5:30/km", "8:00 per mile", "4:45 pace") or a distance loop ("10k loop"); default pace 6:00/km. Run mode: on foot only, no hop limit, 1-minute waypoint stays, legs timed at their pace, rule 13 in the prompt (trails, waterfront, parks; the goose doesn't state distances). Loops are closed by the server (the run back to the first waypoint is added and counted). **Places monthly cap (2026-10-21, deployed).** Google Places searches stop at 990 per Toronto calendar month (`PLACES_MONTHLY_CAP`, default 990), so Google's 1,000 free are never crossed. `public.monthly_quota` + `take_quota(service, cap)` reserve each search atomically before it is sent (tested: 10 simultaneous reservations at a cap of 3 gave exactly 3); `quota_used(service)` is checked at the start of each guide request. At the cap, new requests get "Honk! I've used up all my place searches for this month…" (HTTP 429) and nothing is planned; a request already running when the cap is hit is told searches are unavailable and finishes with what it has. Service role only. The counter was seeded from `api_usage` (65 searches on 2026-10-21; metering began that day, so earlier October searches aren't in it). Optional backstop the owner can set: a daily quota on Places API (New) Text Search in Google Cloud Console (~32/day).
